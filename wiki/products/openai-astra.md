## Summary

Astra is [[openai]]'s next major model family, designed for **long-running, high-difficulty reasoning tasks** and native **multi-agent collaboration** rather than single-shot chat. It uses multiple agents that plan, delegate, revise, and keep working in the background for hours or days on research, coding, and deep analysis. OpenAI has not yet publicly decided whether it will ship as **GPT-6** or as a **GPT-5.x** sub-variant (e.g., GPT-5.7 Sol). No public release date exists — Astra remains an internal model previewed only to select regulators and policymakers in Washington, D.C.

Astra matters because an internal version has reportedly **solved ten previously-open problems** in mathematics and theoretical computer science — problems where central results had seen little or no substantive progress in at least a decade. Highlighted results include proving that **non-sofic groups exist**, building the first symmetry structure that can't be imitated by any finite shuffle, solving **Alain Connes's rigidity conjecture** and **Ehrhart's volume conjecture**, and clearing three problems from **Paul Erdős's list**. Core arguments were generated and organized by Astra, then formalized into machine-checkable **Lean** proof certificates. The total API compute cost across all ten solutions was estimated at **~$2,000** at current [[gpt-5-6]] Sol rates — a step-change price/capability ratio for automated scientific reasoning. OpenAI is reportedly considering **government pre-release review** for Astra given both its capability and emerging U.S./EU regulatory frameworks.
