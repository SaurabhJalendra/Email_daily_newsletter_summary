## Summary

Exo is an open-source distributed AI-inference system that connects computers, smartphones, and other devices into a peer-to-peer cluster. It splits large language models across available hardware so users can run models that would not fit on a single device, positioned on its GitHub repository as a way to *"run frontier AI locally."* Current capabilities include **topology-aware parallelism**, **tensor parallelism**, **MLX-based inference**, and **RDMA over Thunderbolt 5**.

Structurally, Exo challenges the centralized-inference economics of frontier AI: idle consumer hardware can be pooled across a home or local network, improving privacy and lowering hardware barriers. The Nov 2026 cycle anchors Exo's continued expansion toward faster heterogeneous-device inference — including support for newer Apple and NVIDIA hardware and recent work on local AI training and agent optimization. Practical constraints (performance, networking complexity, software compatibility, reliability) remain. Exo sits alongside [[nvidia-pair]] (vendor-coordinated-tier) and [[llm-d]] (Kubernetes-native production-tier) as a third, open-source peer-to-peer tier of the distributed-inference substrate.
