## Summary

Project Panama is an [[anthropic]] initiative to digitize *"all the books in the world"* for AI training. Superhuman HIGH reports that the project has driven a measurable surge in demand for **pre-2022 printed books** — a corpus of interest specifically because it is provably *free from AI-generated text* (post-2022 text corpora are increasingly contaminated by LLM-produced writing, which risks model-collapse dynamics when fed back into training pipelines).

The initiative is structurally significant on two axes: (a) it extends the 2025 [[anthropic]]-Anthropic $1.5B copyright settlement (largest US copyright payout in the industry) from *retrospective compensation for prior training-data ingestion* into *forward-looking large-scale book-digitization program* — implying Anthropic is doubling down on high-quality book corpora as a training-data moat; (b) the *pre-2022* qualifier canonicalizes the *AI-generated-text contamination as a training-data-purity concern* — first publicly framed in this wiki, and pairs with the same-cycle Dwarkesh Patel *"compute 10x more expensive"* essay on why frontier labs will fight harder for scarce compute + scarce clean data alike.
