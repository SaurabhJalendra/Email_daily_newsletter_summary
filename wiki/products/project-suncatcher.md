## Summary

Project Suncatcher is [[google]]'s November 2025 research effort to build machine-learning infrastructure that runs entirely off solar power in space. The program puts TPU-based AI compute on satellites in low-Earth orbit, effectively creating data centers on-orbit that bypass terrestrial grid and cooling constraints. It sits in the same broad "compute supercycle" conversation as hyperscaler terrestrial buildouts, but reframes the problem as an energy-and-location optimization rather than a silicon-supply one.

The project reaches its first operational milestone on **October 1, 2026** with the launch of a refrigerator-sized prototype satellite named **MVP**, aboard SpaceX's Transporter-18 rideshare mission, in partnership with Planet Labs. MVP carries four Google TPUs plus solar panels and supporting hardware. The mission is an engineering validation — not yet an operational orbital data center — testing whether AI chips can operate through launch stresses, radiation, extreme temperatures, and space-based cooling conditions over roughly one year in orbit. Coverage frames MVP as Google's first AI chips in orbit, canonicalizing the Google-side entry into the four-player orbital-AI-DC race alongside [[spacex]] Starmind, [[starcloud]] (already operating a Gemma-on-H100 satellite), and Blue Origin Project Sunrise.
