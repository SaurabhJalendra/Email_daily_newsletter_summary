## Summary

Rubin HPC is a new NVIDIA GPU announced at SC26 (Supercomputing 2026) in mid-November 2026, aimed at agentic workloads, scientific simulation, data processing, engineering, and analytics. It is designed with more than 50,000 CUDA cores, sparse-data acceleration, and seventh-generation NVLink to keep large-scale computations and GPU-to-GPU communication efficient. NVIDIA says the product is expected to become generally available in 2028.

Rubin HPC extends NVIDIA's AI-computing platform beyond model training and inference into scientific HPC: within the broader Vera Rubin architecture, Rubin HPC sits alongside Vera CPUs, networking, interconnects, and software for large AI factories. Early MLPerf results reportedly showed Vera Rubin leading across training, post-training, and agentic-inference benchmarks, and NVIDIA says Rubin systems are entering full production for cloud providers, AI labs, and hyperscalers. The industry significance is that AI agents are being positioned as tools for scientific discovery and HPC, potentially reducing simulation and analysis times for climate research, genomics, and financial modeling, further strengthening NVIDIA's role as the central supplier of AI and accelerated-computing infrastructure.
