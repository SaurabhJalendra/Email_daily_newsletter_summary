## Summary

AI copyright moved from background concern to concrete case law through November 2025. Japanese creative-industry pressure against [[openai]]'s [[sora-2]] continued (Studio Ghibli trade group pushback), a Munich court ruled that ChatGPT illegally trained on song lyrics — one of the first concrete European court losses for a major US frontier lab on training-data grounds — and a German court echoed the ruling later in the month. Meanwhile the publisher-side story accelerated: People Inc. signed an AI-licensing deal with Microsoft's pay-per-use marketplace, [[perplexity]] won a favorable court/licensing ruling and picked up Getty Images as a content partner, and Wikipedia asked AI companies to credit it properly and adopt the paid Wikimedia Enterprise service. The period establishes that (a) training on unlicensed creative works can lose in European courts, (b) licensing marketplaces are emerging as the resolution mechanism, and (c) publishers are actively bargaining rather than only litigating.
