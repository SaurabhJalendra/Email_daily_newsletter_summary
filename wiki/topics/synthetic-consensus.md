## Summary

**Synthetic consensus** is the canonical mid-2026 named-threat-substrate for AI-driven online-manipulation — specifically, coordinated networks of AI personas (*AI swarms*) that combine large language models with multi-agent systems to infiltrate online communities and create the illusion of broad public agreement. Unlike prior individual-bot-tier framings, AI swarms are *coordination-first*: a single operator can run thousands of AI "voices" that adopt local language + tone + interaction patterns, test millions of small-scale persuasion experiments in parallel, and refine their approach in real time based on what's working — making them structurally more effective than traditional bots.

The topic surfaces in mid-2026 alongside the [[ai-slop]] + [[ai-watermarking]] + [[eu-ai-act]] cluster as a *canonical named-threat anchor*. Researchers recommend a four-primitive defense playbook: (1) real-time monitoring of coordination patterns, (2) stronger identity verification for accounts, (3) public incident reporting when swarms are detected, and (4) reducing financial incentives to monetize fake engagement. The framing pairs with the *human-conformity-vulnerability* canonical anchor — humans mimic the group for survival, making them vulnerable to synthetic consensus even when smart and well-informed.
