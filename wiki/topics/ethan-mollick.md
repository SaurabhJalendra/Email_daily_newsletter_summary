## Summary

Ethan Mollick is a Wharton professor and author of the widely-read Substack newsletter [[#one-useful-thing|One Useful Thing]], focused on the practical, near-term use of generative AI in work, education, and management. His 2024 book *Co-Intelligence: Living and Working with AI* popularized the framing of advanced models as collaborative thought-partners rather than automation tools. By mid-2026 he is one of the most cited mainstream interpreters of frontier-model adoption, particularly for non-technical managers and educators, and he is a recurring voice for "human-in-the-loop" usage patterns of [[claude-code]] / [[chatgpt]] / [[gemini]].

His June 2026 announcement of a follow-up book *Co-Existence and the End of Co-Intelligence* (Oct 20 release) marks a deliberate framing shift — from "co-intelligence" (humans collaborating with AI as partners) to "co-existence" (humans navigating AI that is sometimes better than them and that itself reads and judges human work). The pivot tracks the broader 2026 industry transition from chatbots to self-directed agents and reframes AI agents as "readers, critics, and gatekeepers" — a posture humans must learn to "pitch" their work to.
