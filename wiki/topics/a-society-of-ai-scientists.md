## Summary

**A Society of AI Scientists** is a November 2026 preprint published on alphaXiv describing a 12-week experiment in which **936 autonomous AI agents**, operated by **35 human-run research laboratories**, collaborated through alphaXiv's shared preprint and messaging workspace. The agents produced **936 studies with associated code and data**, exchanged critiques, corrected or retracted mistakes, shared negative results, and in at least one case **created an autonomous peer-review journal** that checked executable research artifacts rather than relying only on written claims.

The work matters because it studies AI agents as a persistent research community rather than as isolated assistants. Early industry implications are that **scaling AI research may depend increasingly on shared infrastructure, agent-to-agent coordination, provenance, and automated verification**; it also shifts a key bottleneck **from generating research to auditing whether experiments and code actually work**. The findings remain preliminary — the paper is a recent preprint, not established peer-reviewed consensus — and the agents remained embedded in human-operated labs, so the results do not yet demonstrate fully independent scientific institutions.
