## Summary

Recursive self-improvement (RSI) is the framing — popularized in mainstream AI coverage by [[anthropic]]'s 2026-06-05 essay *"When AI builds itself" / "Sparks of RSI"* — for AI systems that materially accelerate the development of their own successors, eventually approaching a point where a model can design and improve future models with minimal human oversight. Anthropic argues RSI is **not** here yet and **not** inevitable, but says early signs are visible inside their own pipeline: Claude now writes over **80% of merged code** at Anthropic (as of May 2026) and engineers ship roughly **8× more code per quarter** than they did in 2024. Anthropic's policy chief expects AI could begin building its own successors within roughly **two years** and explicitly calls for a coordinated international slowdown (compared to nuclear arms control) — with verification involving both U.S. and Chinese frontier-lab firms — *before* the point of no return. The essay reportedly drew **3M+ views** in its launch day, making it the most-circulated frontier-lab safety post of the year so far.

The framing is paired with [[anthropic]]'s established **AI-safety positioning** (ASL-4 protocol, [[claude-mythos]] controlled release, Anthropic Academy, Pope Leo XIV's *Magnifica Humanitas* endorsement) and arrives at the same cycle as: (a) Anthropic's leaked **Mythos / 'Oceanus' checkpoint** being resold via a Chinese API proxy (a concrete safe-distribution failure), (b) the NSA deploying Mythos for **offensive cyber operations**, and (c) the **CEOs of [[openai]] / [[anthropic]] / Google DeepMind / Microsoft** signing an open letter to Congress on synthetic-DNA bioweapon-design risk. Together the cycle reframes RSI from a thought experiment into a near-term governance problem with named actors.
