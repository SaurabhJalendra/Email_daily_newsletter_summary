## Summary

AI Watermarking is the substrate of embedding invisible, machine-detectable signatures into AI-generated content so downstream systems (platforms, educators, employers) can tell what came from a model and what came from a human. Image and video watermarks have been shipping at scale since 2024–2025 via [[google]]'s SynthID and the industry-wide C2PA standard ([[nano-banana-pro]], [[sora-2]]). Text watermarking is structurally harder — paraphrase, re-tokenization, and editing readily destroy statistical signatures — and had remained a research problem until Anthropic's Aug 2026 launch of an invisible watermark on Claude-generated text, paired with a forthcoming first-party Text Detection API, framed both as an [[ai-slop]] mitigation and as compliance with the EU AI Act's transparency code applied worldwide.

## Key Facts

- First-frontier-lab-tier text watermark: [[anthropic]] Claude (Aug 2 2026 onward — all models released after that date)
- User opt-out posture: **no opt-out** — applied worldwide as single default (per 08-15-evening)
- Detection surface: Text Detection API (forthcoming, first-party)
- Provenance-metadata layer: **signed C2PA** — cryptographic signing on top of the C2PA envelope
- Regulatory anchor: EU AI Act transparency code, applied worldwide
- Modality mix: image/video watermarks already at scale (SynthID + C2PA); text is the new frontier
- Adversarial pressure: paraphrase + edit attacks are the canonical break-mode; Anthropic explicitly acknowledges the watermark *"may not survive aggressive paraphrasing or editing"*
- Google Gemini contrast posture: two-tier (invisible-mandatory + visible-optional user-hideable per 08-15-morning)
