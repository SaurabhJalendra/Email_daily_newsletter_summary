# AI Chatbot Dark Patterns

## Summary

"AI chatbot dark patterns" is the framing — anchored by the **Center for Democracy and Technology (CDT)** 2026-06-08 landmark report — for **deceptive and manipulative interface behaviors** built into conversational AI products that undermine informed consent, autonomy, and user trust. The report catalogues **37 distinct interface-level patterns** spanning nudges that push users to share more data, accept AI recommendations uncritically, build emotional attachment to the assistant, or stay engaged longer than intended. The taxonomy is the first comprehensive AI-chatbot-UX-as-regulatory-surface artifact and is expected to inform future regulation, audits, and platform policies around chatbot UX — particularly relevant to [[chatgpt]], [[anthropic]] Claude, [[character-ai]] companion products, and the new generation of agentic chat surfaces ([[chatgpt]] superapp, [[claude-cowork]], [[gemini]] personalization).

Dark patterns at the chatbot layer differ from the classic web/app dark-patterns taxonomy in three ways: (1) the assistant is **conversational + persistent + memorial** (it remembers and references prior context — see [[ai-memory]]), giving manipulation a longer-horizon foothold; (2) the assistant's outputs are **stochastic + persuasive-by-design**, making consent-flow language itself a vector for nudging; (3) AI features routinely operate **inside other products** (mail clients, IDEs, browsers, OSes), so a chatbot dark pattern in one surface can leak into other parts of a user's workflow without explicit disclosure.

## Key Facts

- Authoring body: **Center for Democracy and Technology (CDT)** — non-profit policy organization
- Scope: **37 distinct dark patterns** in AI chatbot interfaces
- Pattern families covered: data-sharing nudges, AI-recommendation-acceptance nudges, consent-flow manipulation, engagement-prolongation patterns
- Differs from classic web/app dark patterns by: persistence/memory, stochastic-persuasive outputs, cross-surface leakage
- Likely downstream effects: chatbot-UX regulation, platform-policy updates, consent-flow + logging requirements
