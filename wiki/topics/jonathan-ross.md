## Summary

Jonathan Ross is the founder of [[groq]], the inventor of Google's Tensor Processing Unit (TPU) — the chip that powered DeepMind's AlphaGo — and, as of the December 2025 [[groq]]-[[nvidia]] deal, **Chief Software Architect at NVIDIA** focused on "doubling the world's AI compute" by integrating Groq's ultra-fast inference technology with NVIDIA's GPU platforms. His work centers on specialized inference chips (Groq's **LPU**, now productized inside NVIDIA's **LPX platform**) designed for very low-latency, high-throughput AI workloads — an architecture Ross frames as complementary to GPUs rather than competitive ("GPUs are 18-wheelers, LPUs are delivery vans" in a compute-logistics analogy).

Ross became a central figure in AI/ML news after closing the roughly **$20 billion Groq-NVIDIA deal** — which went from an initial phone call about buying ~100,000 GPUs to NVIDIA's largest deal ever in about **three weeks**. The agreement is structured as a non-exclusive licensing deal for Groq's inference technology plus a major transfer of Groq talent — including Ross — into NVIDIA ("an acqui-hire in all but name"), while GroqCloud continues to operate independently.
