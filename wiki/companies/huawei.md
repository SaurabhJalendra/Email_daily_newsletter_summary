## Summary

Huawei is a Chinese technology conglomerate whose Ascend AI accelerator line is the leading domestic alternative to NVIDIA in China's AI infrastructure build-out, paired with its networking stack, software ecosystem, and large-scale data-center deployments. The flagship Horinger data center in Inner Mongolia, analyzed via satellite imagery by Epoch AI in Nov 2026, offers a concrete yardstick for China's sovereign-compute posture: an estimated 286 MW of IT power capacity, approximately 105,000 Ascend 910C and 8,000 Ascend 910B chips operational, totaling roughly 81,000 H100-equivalents (90% CI: 36,000-141,000) — only about 7% of the compute hosted at SpaceX's Colossus 2 facility in Tennessee.

The analysis crystallizes the China–US AI compute gap: Huawei's Ascend 910C performs at ~0.76 H100e per chip while NVIDIA's B200/B300 deliver ~2.5 H100e, so even with abundant energy and rapid data-center construction, Chinese facilities support only ~40% of the compute per equivalent power draw of leading US sites. Horinger is expected to grow AI compute by over 70% by early 2028 (at least three new buildings planned beyond the six already built), but China's compute constraints are unlikely to ease over the next two years — a structural headwind for Chinese frontier-AI capabilities and deployment capacity.
