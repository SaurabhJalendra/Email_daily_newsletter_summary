## Summary

**SF Compute** is a US-based AI-compute marketplace building what it calls a **"programmable data center"** — a market exchange for buyers and sellers to trade compute hours with multiple delivery and settlement primitives. The company's canonical wiki-tier signal is the Oct 2026 Latent.Space podcast appearance by CEO **Evan Conrad**, which documents four concrete substrate-tier anchors: (i) **physical settlement** — buyers receive usable compute resources rather than just cash against an index; (ii) **multi-constraint order book** — customers can specify GPU type, memory, storage, and other compute-resource parameters; (iii) **cluster delivery pipeline** — SF Compute has built the entire operating capacity of a cloud including site visits, bill-of-materials design, and supply-chain delivery, and works with partners to turn data centers into GPU clusters; (iv) **InfiniBand-subdivide-on-the-fly** — the fabric can be dynamically subdivided, making the exchange viable for platform-as-a-service tenants.

The company has closed **several hundred million dollars of volume** and has a **significant number of new clusters** in the pipeline. SF Compute also works with sovereign-compute partners to set up clouds in their own countries, placing it alongside the [[humain]] + [[nscale]] + [[aleph-alpha]] sovereign-compute canonical arc.

Structurally, SF Compute sits on the compute-market-exchange substrate — distinct from the NVIDIA → hyperscaler → tenant GPU-rental canonical substrate dominated by AWS/Azure/GCP and the Neo-Cloud cohort ([[baseten]] + [[fireworks]] + [[together-ai]] + [[nebius]]). The "programmable data center" framing productizes compute as a tradeable, multi-constraint-order-booked, physically-settled asset class rather than a bilateral rental agreement.
