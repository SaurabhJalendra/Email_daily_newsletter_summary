## Summary

**Tenstorrent** is an AI-computing and semiconductor startup led by veteran processor architect **Jim Keller** (ex-AMD/Apple/Tesla/Intel). It develops four product categories: **AI accelerators**, **CPUs**, **open software**, and **Galaxy server systems** — designed to run inference workloads including LLMs, coding assistants, chatbots, and video generation. Its go-to-market strategy combines **selling AI systems** with **licensing chip and CPU intellectual property** to organizations designing their own silicon, positioning Tenstorrent as a hybrid vendor/IP-licensor alternative to NVIDIA's largely dominant ecosystem.

Tenstorrent's flagship **Blackhole** chips run the **BlockFloat8** number format natively and are priced around **$1,000 per chip** — compared to GPUs with native support that list near **$40,000**, implying a ~**40× price advantage at the same numerics format**. The Oct 19 2026 **$1.5B Series E at $6.2B post-money valuation** more than doubles Tenstorrent's reported late-2024 valuation; the company says proceeds will support volume manufacturing, global expansion, Galaxy server deployments, and further AI + CPU IP development.

Structurally, Tenstorrent sits in the broader *non-NVIDIA AI-silicon cohort* alongside [[amd]] + [[openai-jalapeno]] (OpenAI custom silicon) + Google Frozen v2 + Microsoft Maia 300 + Meta Iris + AWS Trainium + [[taalas]] (AMD-acquired custom-inference-ASIC). Reports earlier in 2026 said **Intel** and **Qualcomm** separately explored acquiring Tenstorrent — Qualcomm-related reports cited a possible valuation of roughly $8B-$10B — but Tenstorrent chose independence via the Series E, graduating from "potential acquisition target" into "concrete-independent-financed-tier."
