## 📌 If You Only Read One Thing

The single most important development of the 2026-04-09 → 2026-04-15 window is that *the AI industry stopped debating agent orchestration and ratified it as the default.* Inside one calendar week, every frontier lab — [[anthropic]], [[openai]], [[google]], [[meta]], and [[xai]] — and at least two developer-tool incumbents — [[cursor]] and [[vercel]] — shipped, announced, or staged a fleet-of-agents surface where the user is no longer typing alongside one model but supervising a parallel pool of them. [[anthropic]] launched [[claude-managed-agents]] on 2026-04-10 as a fully-managed cloud agent runtime, then on 2026-04-14 added Ultraplan as a cloud-based collaborative task-planning companion, put [[claude-code]] Coordinator Mode into development as an explicit Claude-as-orchestrator surface, and announced a desktop overhaul. [[openai]] countered on 2026-04-14 with a unified Codex app whose Scratchpad runs parallel Codex tasks from a single UI, while landing a Tubi native app *inside* ChatGPT. [[google]] shipped PaperOrchestra (multi-agent LaTeX research-paper workflow) the same day and on 2026-04-15 updated [[gemini-enterprise]]'s desktop Agent surface plus a fleet-of-coding-agents feature. [[xai]] disclosed Grok Build credits for an upcoming launch with both a local CLI and remote web interface. [[cursor]] 3.1 made fleet-of-agents management a first-class IDE surface on 2026-04-15. [[vercel]] open-sourced Open Agents — a cloud coding-agent platform built to run hundreds of agents simultaneously.

The strategic implication is structural rather than incremental. Single-agent pair-programming has visibly retreated from public roadmaps, and the metaphor every major vendor now uses for itself is *not* "an AI that helps you code" but "a fleet you dispatch and supervise." Anthropic's Coordinator Mode framing makes the new role hierarchy explicit: Claude is the engineering manager, not the IC, delegating to parallel sub-agents. Within a single business week the entire developer-AI conversation slid one layer up the abstraction stack, and the strategic question stopped being which model writes the best code and became which platform best schedules, supervises, and reconciles a parallel fleet. Because every major vendor has now staked out a public position, the category is at the inflection point where *naming* and *default UI primitives* — Coordinator vs. Scratchpad vs. fleet vs. Open Agents vs. PaperOrchestra — become the load-bearing strategic decisions for the next 6–12 months. The lab that wins the default term likely wins category leadership a quarter out.

The second thing worth reading about is [[claude-mythos]] — not because it shipped, but because over these same seven days [[anthropic]] made it crystal clear that it will *not* ship publicly, and that the reason is cybersecurity. On 2026-04-09 Anthropic announced [[project-glasswing]], a cybersecurity coalition with AWS, Apple, Google, Microsoft, and NVIDIA as partners using an unreleased Mythos Preview to detect and fix vulnerabilities at scale. The same day, Mythos SWE-bench numbers surfaced for the first time — 93.9% SWE-bench Verified and 77.8% SWE-bench Pro — placing the unreleased internal model comfortably above [[claude-opus-4-7]]. On 2026-04-10 Anthropic stated explicitly that Mythos "will not be released publicly due to concerns over accelerating cyberattacks." On 2026-04-13 TAAFT published a counter-framing claiming "Project Glasswing" was actually a hacker group that had breached Mythos — a contradiction this wiki preserves as an open question on both pages pending a third-mention resolution. On 2026-04-15 regulators warned major banks about cybersecurity risks linked to Mythos Preview — the first regulatory-posture signal against a Mythos deployment in financial services. Taken together, this is the clearest case study yet of a frontier lab choosing *not* to release its best model and instead monetizing it through a governance coalition. If the template works for Anthropic, expect [[openai]], [[google]], and [[meta]] to deploy similar framings around their own next-generation capabilities within two quarters. The "too dangerous to release" posture has graduated from rhetoric into a repeatable go-to-market motion.
