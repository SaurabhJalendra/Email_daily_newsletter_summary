## Summary

Open-source models — specifically Chinese open-weight foundation models (Zhipu's GLM, Alibaba's Qwen, DeepSeek, MiniMax, Moonshot's Kimi K2 lineage) and independent Western releases (Meta's earlier Llama, French Mistral) — closed the frontier quality gap through 2026. By mid-2026 they achieve competitive results on named coding + agent + reasoning benchmarks at roughly 1/6 to 1/10 the cost of frontier closed-API flagships. The immediate strategic consequence: [[ai-moats]] shifted from *"the model"* to workflow, orchestration, data accumulation, trust, and cost-routing.

The regulatory consequence, canonicalized in Nathan Lambert's Interconnects essay "6 months to live for open models," is that the same capability-crossing is the primary driver of a new US regulatory response — a White House executive order under discussion that would treat open-weights models (particularly Chinese-origin) as a distinct strategic-asset category needing separate policy treatment from closed-API frontier models. Lambert's critique: the emerging regulatory framework is *regulatory capture* by frontier-labs (specifically [[anthropic]]) pushing for policies that benefit their closed-API businesses; APIs are not magically secure, and unilateral US action cannot ceiling global open-source progress absent a multilateral agreement.

- Named canonical open-weights family cohort (2026-07 essay-tier): [[glm-5-2]] (Zhipu), Qwen (Alibaba), [[minimax]], DeepSeek, Kimi K2 (Moonshot)
- Cost-differential anchor: open-weights competitive at ~1/6 to 1/10 the cost of frontier closed APIs (GLM-5.2 $4.40 vs Sol $30 per M output tokens)
- Named benchmarks where open-weights show competitive parity: SWE-bench Pro (MiniMax + GLM-5.2), FrontierSWE (GLM-5.2 74.4% vs GPT-5.5 72.6%), 1524 Elo on Artificial Analysis Index (GLM-5.2)
- Regulatory instrument under discussion: White House executive order on open models, potentially targeting Chinese-origin models and government uses (per Nathan Lambert essay)
- Anthropic's role per Lambert: framed as *pushing for regulations benefiting its own products* — an explicit regulatory-capture critique
- Companion product motion: US hyperscaler open-weights posture is contested — [[meta]] pivoted away from open-weights in Muse Spark era; Microsoft has no open-weights flagship
