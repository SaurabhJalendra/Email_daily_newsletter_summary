# AI Regulation

> **Type**: trend
> **First mentioned**: 2025-10-17
> **Last updated**: 2025-10-17
> **Status**: active
> **Related**: [[openai]], [[sora-2]], [[ai-video-generation]]

## Summary

In mid-October 2025 external pressure on AI labs intensified along two independent axes: the Japanese government formally asked [[openai]] to stop generating manga/anime lookalikes via [[sora-2]], and the AFL-CIO (representing ~15M US workers) launched a "Workers First Initiative on AI" demanding labor protections, retraining, and transparency. Both events point to a widening consensus that the IP-and-labor consequences of generative AI require policy response, not just industry self-regulation.

## Key Facts

- Two pressure vectors emerging in Oct 2025: IP (copyright of likenesses) and labor (worker-centered AI)
- Japan is first major government to formally escalate against OpenAI on generative-video IP

## Open Questions

- Does Japan's request carry legal weight, or is it advisory pressure?
- Will the AFL-CIO initiative translate into federal AI-labor legislation?
- Will other nations follow Japan on generative-video copyright?
