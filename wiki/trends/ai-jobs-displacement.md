## Summary

AI jobs displacement collects hard-data points on how generative AI is reshaping labor markets — separate from rhetoric or vendor hype. The central instrument is [[anthropic]]'s Economic Index, which uses observed usage patterns to gauge real exposure by occupation. Early findings (published March 2026) put 44% of jobs at high risk of automation, with computer programmers, customer-service reps, and data-entry workers the most exposed. Entry-level compression is already measurable: hiring of 22–25-year-olds into exposed fields fell 14% since ChatGPT's late-2022 launch. The trend also tracks flagship layoffs tied directly to AI agents, such as [[block]]'s ~50% workforce cut citing internal agent "Goose."

## Key Facts

- Anthropic Economic Index (Mar 2026): 44% of jobs high-risk; 74.5% programmer task coverage
- Gap: theoretical automation (94% of computer/math tasks) vs real-world usage (33%) = 61-point headroom
- Entry-level hit: 22–25-year-old hiring in exposed fields −14% vs pre-ChatGPT baseline
- Flagship layoff tied to agent: Block's Goose-cited workforce cut (Mar 2026)
